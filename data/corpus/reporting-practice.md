# Reporting practice for research artifacts

## Structure a reader can act on

A research report answers four things in order: what was measured, what the corpus
says, what remains unknown, and what the reader should not conclude. Headings in that
order let a reader stop early and still have the honest version.

The open questions section is not filler. Naming what the data cannot show is what
makes the measured section trustworthy, because it proves the author knew the
boundary rather than ignoring it.

## Language discipline

A report describes what the data shows and avoids instructions. There is no version of
this tool that should tell a reader to buy or sell, and the composer is prompted to
refuse that framing. The disclaimer is not a substitute for that restraint.

Direction words are only used when a computed metric supports them. A measured move
of twelve percent is a measurement. Calling the same move bullish is an opinion with a
number attached to it, which is the worst of both.

## Reproducibility

Every report states which data source produced it, when the computation ran, and
which window the metrics cover. Without those three, a number cannot be checked, and
an unchecked number is decoration.

When a source was unavailable and a fallback answered, the report says which source
answered. Silent fallback is how a stale snapshot gets quoted as live data.
